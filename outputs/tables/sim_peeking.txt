  There is no effect in this data. Any 'significant' result is a false
  positive by construction. Testing once behaves as advertised (4.8%).
  Testing ten times as the data arrives finds a spurious winner 20% of
  the time -- and the analyst who stops at the first significant look has
  no way of telling that is what happened.

==========================================================================
2. CORRECTED: O'Brien-Fleming alpha-spending boundary
==========================================================================

  The fix is to spend the error budget across the looks rather than
  charging the full 5% at each one. O'Brien-Fleming spends very little
  early -- an early stop must clear a high bar -- and approaches the
  nominal alpha at the final look.

    look    n/arm   info frac   z boundary    ~nominal p
       1    2,000        0.10        6.477      9.38e-11
       2    4,000        0.20        4.580      4.66e-06
       3    6,000        0.30        3.739      1.85e-04
       4    8,000        0.40        3.238      1.20e-03
       5   10,000        0.50        2.896      3.77e-03
       6   12,000        0.60        2.644      8.19e-03
       7   14,000        0.70        2.448      1.44e-02
       8   16,000        0.80        2.290      2.20e-02
       9   18,000        0.90        2.159      3.09e-02
      10   20,000        1.00        2.048      4.06e-02

  False-positive rate with the boundary : 5.05%   (target 5%)
  Compare uncorrected                   : 19.65%

  Read the boundary column. To stop at the FIRST look you would need
  p < 9.4e-11 -- not p < 0.05. That is the price of holding
  the option to stop early, and it is a steep one at 10% of the data.
  By the final look the bar has relaxed to p < 0.0406, just
  inside the nominal 0.05, which is the characteristic OBF shape.

  This is why the plan fixes a single analysis: it buys the full 0.05 at
  the one moment that matters, rather than rationing it across looks nobody
  committed to in advance.

==========================================================================
3. WHY THE PLAN SAYS 'NO PEEKING'
==========================================================================

  Section 3 fixes one analysis on the full dataset after validation passes.
  For this dataset that is enforced by construction -- it arrives as a
  completed snapshot -- but the rule is stated because it is the rule that
  would apply to a live test.

  The alternative is not 'never look'. It is: decide the looks in advance
  and spend alpha across them, as in part 2. What is not available is
  looking whenever you like and applying 0.05 each time, which is the
  3.9x inflation in part 1.
